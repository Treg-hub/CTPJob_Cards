The Geofence — How We Know You're On Site
Automatic

What it does

An ~400 m boundary around the site (default). The moment you cross in: you get "Arrived On-Site" and jobs start routing to you.

Cross out: "Left Site Area". Jobs stop routing to you.

All automatic — as long as Location is "Allow All the Time" and battery optimisation is off.

What it's NOT

✗ Not used to track movements off site
✗ Not used to log where you eat lunch
✗ Not used to "check on you"

It only ever checks: inside the boundary or outside the boundary. That's it.

Two reasons it exists: a technician at home shouldn't be alerted to a breakdown. And if you're off site, job alerts wait in your in-app inbox instead of pushing — the server won't fire a P5 alarm at someone who can't attend.
Anatomy of a Job Card
▶ LIVE DEMO

When operator / foreman creates

  • Department → Area → Machine → Part
  • Description of the fault
  • Priority 1–5
  • Type: Mechanical / Electrical / Mech-Elec

When technician works & closes

  • Notes — running observations, parts used, follow-ups
  • Photos — before, after, part numbers, damage
  • Complete (Corrective Action) — what you actually did to resolve the fault. Required at closure.
Both sides need the full picture: operators describe the fault; technicians record cause and cure. Foremen — same create path, plus keep an eye on quality of cards from your area. Skip a field or write nothing in Complete — that detail is lost forever.

Fleet reports
Minor
Small fault — the machine can still be used.
Normal
Needs attention soon — can still work for now.
Urgent
Serious fault — the mechanic should prioritise it.

The Daily Check & What Happens Next
Fleet reports

Before you use a machine

A quick pre-use safety check — tyres, forks, brakes, leaks — plus the hour-meter reading.

Tick anything as faulty and it becomes a report automatically. No double work.

After you send a report

It appears on the mechanic's To Fix list. Out of Service marks the machine down so no one else takes it.

You can see the status of everything you've reported — open, in hand, or fixed.

Your report and your daily check are the machine's logbook. A "grinding noise" today is the bearing we catch before it strands the forklift next week.

Why Accurate Notes Matter
Live today + where we're heading

1. Live — similar jobs

When someone opens a job on the same machine / part, the app can surface related past jobs so the next tech starts with real history — not guesswork.

"Fixed it" = they get nothing useful. Real detail = a head start.

2. Live — reoccurrence

Pulse tracks repeat faults. Managers can see patterns and decide on PM — your notes are the evidence behind those decisions.

Automated “AI pushes a PM to the workshop manager” is not live yet.

3. Heading — fault assistant

An in-app assistant trained on closure notes is on the roadmap — so a tech can ask what was done last time.

Wrote nothing? Future tools will have nothing to learn from at 3am.

The deal: 60 extra seconds at closure. In return — fewer repeat breakdowns, and the next person gets your solution on screen.
